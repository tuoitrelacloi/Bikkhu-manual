# resolve # intention ✨
**“Right Intention” (Sammā-saṅkappa)** is explained as the second factor of the Noble Eightfold Path. It’s not just about having _any_ intention, but specifically cultivating wholesome mental orientations.

### 🎯 Right Intention focuses on three key directions:

- **Renunciation (Nekkhama-saṅkappa)** → Intention to let go of craving and attachment, moving toward simplicity and freedom.
    
- **Non-ill will (Abyāpāda-saṅkappa)** → Intention of kindness, compassion, and goodwill, instead of anger or hostility.
    
- **Non-harming (Avihiṃsā-saṅkappa)** → Intention to avoid cruelty or violence, fostering harmlessness toward all beings.
    

### 🧘 Context in practice

- In meditation, it’s not about “thinking good thoughts” but about setting the _mental orientation_ of the mind — leaning toward peace, compassion, and clarity.
    
- In daily life, Right Intention guides ethical choices: for example, choosing actions motivated by compassion rather than greed or revenge.
    

So, when you ask “intention on what?” — it’s intention directed toward **renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness**, the three wholesome aims that shape the path
