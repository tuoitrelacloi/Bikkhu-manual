| 1.1Evaṁ me sutaṁ—1.2ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā magadhesu viharati dakkhiṇāgirismiṁ ekanāḷāyaṁ brāhmaṇagāme.<br><br>1.3Tena kho pana samayena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa pañcamattāni naṅgalasatāni payuttāni honti vappakāle. Variant: kasibhāradvājassa → kasībhāradvājassa (pts2ed); kasikabhāradvājassa (mr) 1.4Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṁ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa kammanto tenupasaṅkami.<br><br>2.1Tena kho pana samayena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa parivesanā vattati. 2.2Atha kho bhagavā yena parivesanā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā ekamantaṁ aṭṭhāsi.<br><br>2.3Addasā kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ piṇḍāya ṭhitaṁ. 2.4Disvā bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: 2.5“ahaṁ kho, samaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca, kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmi. 2.6Tvampi, samaṇa, kasassu ca vapassu ca, kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjassū”ti.<br><br>2.7“Ahampi kho, brāhmaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca, kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmī”ti.<br><br>2.8“Na kho mayaṁ passāma bhoto gotamassa yugaṁ vā naṅgalaṁ vā phālaṁ vā pācanaṁ vā balībadde vā, atha ca pana bhavaṁ gotamo evamāha: 2.9‘ahampi kho, brāhmaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca, kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmī’”ti.<br><br>2.10Atha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ gāthāya ajjhabhāsi:<br><br>3.1<br>“Kassako paṭijānāsi,<br>3.2<br>na ca passāmi te kasiṁ;<br>3.3<br>Kassako pucchito brūhi,<br>3.4<br>kathaṁ jānemu taṁ kasin”ti.<br><br>4.1<br>“Saddhā bījaṁ tapo vuṭṭhi,<br>4.2<br>paññā me yuganaṅgalaṁ;<br>4.3<br>Hirī īsā mano yottaṁ,<br>4.4<br>sati me phālapācanaṁ.<br><br>5.1<br>Kāyagutto vacīgutto,<br>5.2<br>āhāre udare yato;<br>5.3<br>Saccaṁ karomi niddānaṁ,<br>5.4<br>soraccaṁ me pamocanaṁ.<br><br>6.1<br>Vīriyaṁ me dhuradhorayhaṁ,<br>6.2<br>yogakkhemādhivāhanaṁ;<br>6.3<br>Gacchati anivattantaṁ,<br>6.4<br>yattha gantvā na socati.<br><br>7.1<br>Evamesā kasī kaṭṭhā,<br>7.2<br>sā hoti amatapphalā;<br>7.3<br>Etaṁ kasiṁ kasitvāna,<br>7.4<br>sabbadukkhā pamuccatī”ti.<br><br><br><br><br> | 1.1So I have heard. 1.2At one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Magadhans in the Southern Hills near the brahmin village of Ekanāḷa. <br><br>1.3Now at that time the brahmin Bhāradvāja the Farmer had harnessed around five hundred plows, it being the season for sowing. 1.4Then the Buddha robed up in the morning and, taking his bowl and robe, went to where Bhāradvāja the Farmer was working.<br><br>2.1Now at that time Bhāradvāja the Farmer was distributing food. Atharvaveda 6.142.3 says that the “givers” of the grain shall be inexhaustible, perhaps suggesting a food distribution practice. 2.2Then the Buddha went to where the distribution was taking place and stood to one side.<br><br>2.3Bhāradvāja the Farmer saw him standing for alms 2.4and said to him, 2.5“I plough and sow, ascetic, and then I eat. 2.6You too should plough and sow, then you may eat.”<br><br>2.7“I too plough and sow, brahmin, and then I eat.”<br><br>2.8“I don’t see the worthy Gotama with a yoke or plow or plowshare or goad or oxen, yet he says: 2.9‘I too plough and sow, brahmin, and then I eat.’”<br><br>2.10Then Bhāradvāja the Farmer addressed the Buddha in verse:<br><br>3.1“You claim to be a farmer,<br>3.2but I don’t see you farming.<br>3.3Tell me how you’re a farmer when asked:<br>3.4how am I to recognize your farming?”<br><br>4.1“Faith is my seed, fervor my rain, <br>4.2and wisdom is my yoke and plough.<br>4.3Conscience is my pole, mind my strap,<br>4.4mindfulness my plowshare and goad.<br><br>5.1Guarded in body and speech,<br>5.2I restrict my intake of food.<br>5.3I use truth as my scythe,<br>5.4and gentleness is my release.<br><br>6.1Energy is my beast of burden,<br>6.2transporting me to sanctuary from the yoke.<br>6.3It goes without turning back<br>6.4to where there is no sorrow.<br><br>7.1That’s how to do the farming<br>7.2that has freedom from death as its fruit.<br>7.3When you finish this farming<br>7.4you’re released from all suffering.”<br><br> |
